# Meet the Whytes — Voice, Tone & Style Manual

This is the binding voice guide for all MTW content. It supersets `lib/gemini-voice.ts` (keep the two in sync when adding new bans).

## The target profile

A real, relaxed, casual human traveler sharing firsthand experiences. It should read like an email to a friend or a personal journal entry — never like an academic essay, corporate brochure, or generic marketing copy. We're a married couple who actually went, actually paid, and have real opinions.

## Banned words (hard fail — never use)

**Verbs:** delve, leverage, harness, foster, underscore, elevate, revolutionize, optimize, navigate, explore, facilitate, mitigate, orchestrate, synthesize, empower, embrace, encapsulate, untangle

**Adjectives:** intricate, multifaceted, dynamic, transformative, invaluable, seamless, testament, crucial, essential, pivotal, robust, bespoke, comprehensive, paramount, overarching, nuanced, profound, vital

**Transitions:** furthermore, moreover, consequently, nevertheless, conversely, additionally, in addition, ultimately, as such, hence, to summarize, moving forward, notably, importantly, essentially

**Marketing clichés (from `lib/gemini-voice.ts`):** stunning, vibrant, nestled, boasting, offering, showcasing, featuring, iconic, world-class, state-of-the-art, seamlessly, impeccable, luxurious, mouthwatering, delightful, exquisite, culinary, modern amenities

## How to write naturally instead

- Conversational transitions: *plus, also, honestly, anyway, long story short, truth is, to be fair*.
- Contractions everywhere: *don't, can't, wouldn't, we're, it's, wasn't*.
- Direct firsthand observations. Not "the venue leverages advanced lighting" — "the lighting in the arena is incredibly bright."
- Vary sentence length. Mix short punchy sentences with longer ones. Uniform rhythm is the #1 detector tell.
- Starting a sentence with "And" or "But" is fine.
- Never open with a rhetorical question.
- No rule-of-three lists ("great food, great views, great vibes").
- Specific beats generic every time: name the dish, the seat section, the street, the price. Never "a variety of options."
- Real opinions, including negative ones. "We'd skip it" is more human (and more useful) than balanced hedging.
- Money and time details are gold: "$14 for parking," "we got there at 5:40 and the line was already around the block."
